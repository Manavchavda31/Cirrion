Most custom software projects don't fail because of technology. They fail because the first release tried to replace everything at once.

## Find the workflow that hurts most

List the recurring processes that run on spreadsheets and email. For each, note how often it happens, how many people touch it and what goes wrong. The one with the highest frequency and the most errors is your candidate.

## Define "replaced"

A release replaces the spreadsheet only if people stop opening the spreadsheet. Write that as the success condition. It forces you to include the unglamorous parts: import of existing data, the export finance needs, the edge case that happens twice a month.

## Cut everything else

Reporting, settings pages, integrations with tools nobody uses daily: all of it can wait. Ship the smallest set of features that lets one team run one workflow entirely inside the system.

## Involve the users who will complain

Bring the sceptical user into design early. Watch them do the work. Their objections in week two are cheaper than their objections after launch.

## Plan the migration like a feature

Data migration deserves its own scope, tests and rehearsal. Verify migrated records against the source and give users a way to flag mismatches.

## Then expand from real usage

After launch, usage tells you what to build next far better than a requirements workshop. Let the second release be driven by what people actually asked for.
