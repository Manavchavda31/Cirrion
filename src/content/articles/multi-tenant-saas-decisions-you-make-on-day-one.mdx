Multi-tenancy is one of those architecture decisions that is cheap to make early and painful to change once customers depend on it.

## Choose an isolation model deliberately

There are three common approaches:

- **Shared schema, tenant column**: simplest and cheapest to run. Isolation depends on every query being scoped correctly.
- **Schema per tenant**: stronger separation and easier per-tenant operations, with more migration overhead.
- **Database per tenant**: strongest isolation, highest operational cost.

Pick based on data sensitivity, expected tenant count and compliance requirements, not on habit.

## Enforce isolation below the application

If isolation relies on developers remembering a filter, it will eventually fail. Use database-level controls, such as row-level security, so a missing filter returns nothing instead of leaking data.

## Model roles and permissions early

Decide how users, roles and tenants relate before you build features. Retrofitting permissions into an existing data model touches everything.

## Build billing as a system

Plans, trials, usage and invoices become tangled quickly when added later. Treat billing as its own module with clear events, and reconcile it against your payment provider.

## Add tenant-aware observability

Logs and metrics should be filterable by tenant. When a customer reports a problem, you want to see their traffic in seconds.

## Give your team admin tooling

Support staff need safe ways to inspect and assist tenants, with audit logs. Building this early prevents dangerous shortcuts later.

## Keep the first release small

None of this means building everything at once. It means making the few foundational decisions deliberately, then shipping a focused product on top of them.
